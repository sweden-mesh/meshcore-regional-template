This page is for you who own a house, a barn, a mast or a hilltop where someone wants to set
up a repeater for the MeshCore network in {{< region "name" >}}. It is written so it can be printed
and handed over: everything important fits on one A4 page.

If you want to offer a site yourself, there is more in [Host a node](/bli-nodvard/).

## What is being set up? {#vad-är-det-som-sätts-upp}

A repeater is a small radio that receives short text messages and sends them on, so that
they reach farther. It is needed high up, because radio waves on this frequency travel best with a clear
line of sight. That is why your site in particular is of interest.

- **A box** about the size of a soap dish, and **an antenna** a few tens of centimeters long.
- **A mount**, for example a clamp on a pipe or a screw in a wall, like a TV antenna mount.
- **A solar panel** if there is no power. The panel is often smaller than an A4 sheet. There are also
  [ready-made solar repeaters](/bli-nodvard/#fardiga-solrepeatrar) with everything in one box.

## Power and cost {#ström-och-kostnad}

A repeater draws between a few hundredths of a watt and half a watt, depending on which board it has.
That comes to at most around 9 kWh a year, a few tens of kronor. With a solar panel it costs you nothing.

## Radio {#radio}

- The repeater transmits on 869.4–869.65 MHz, a band that is free to use without a license throughout the EU.
  In Sweden the rules of the Swedish Post and Telecom Authority (PTS) apply to the band.
- It transmits with **at most half a watt** (500 mW ERP) and **at most a tenth of the time**, in short
  bursts. Usually it is much less.
- At a distance of one meter, the radiation while transmitting is below 2 percent of the reference level that
  applies to the general public (the EU recommendation and the regulations of the Swedish Radiation Safety Authority, Strålsäkerhetsmyndigheten). Averaged
  over time it is below 0.2 percent. A mobile phone held to your ear gives more.

## Who owns it and who is responsible? {#vem-äger-och-vem-ansvarar}

- **The equipment is owned by whoever sets it up**, not by you. It is a good idea to write down who that is and how
  to reach them.
- **Agree on responsibility** for damage to the equipment and for damage it could cause.
  The repeater's owner can check what their own home insurance covers. You can ask your
  insurance company whether it covers your roof or your mast.
- **Mounting** is done as for a TV antenna: firmly, without damaging the roof, and in a way you both
  approve. If the antenna is at the very top of a building, the same advice about lightning applies as for other antennas.

## Access and removal {#tillträde-och-borttagning}

- You decide together **when and how** someone may come to the site, for setup, maintenance
  and removal. Usually one visit is needed for the setup and rarely anything after that.
- **You can ask to have it removed whenever you want.** Agree on how long that may take.

## Nothing is collected about you {#det-samlas-inget-om-dig}

The repeater only passes on what it hears and stores nothing. It needs no internet and
cannot get into your network. If the owner wants it to also show on the map it needs Wi-Fi, but only if you
agree to that.
